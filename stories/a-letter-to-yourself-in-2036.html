The Future

A Letter to Yourself in 2036

Ten years from now, a stranger will open an envelope with your name on it. The stranger will be you. Here is what to tell them — and what to ask.

With Time · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Dear You, ten years from now — let us begin with the thing you most need to hear and will least believe: I do not know how any of it turned out. From here, in 2026, your life is still all questions. That job, that worry, that person, that hope you are almost embarrassed to say out loud — from this side of the decade, every one of them is still unwritten. You are the only person alive who knows the answers. Please be gentle with them, and with the one who asked.

Here is what I hope for you, specifically and on the record. I hope the people we love are still within reach, and that we told them so while it was easy. I hope we finally took the trip. I hope we kept the friend, forgave the debt, finished the project, planted the thing. I hope the worry that kept us up this year turned out to be the small one. Most of all, I hope we did not postpone joy until the conditions were perfect, because from here, the conditions look suspiciously like an excuse we have used before.

And here is what I ask of you, since a letter should carry weight in both directions. Do not mock me. The version of you reading this was doing their best with half the information and most of the fear. If I got things wrong, remember that I got them wrong in good faith, at full effort, on your behalf. Every mistake in your past was made by someone who was trying to build you a future. You are standing in it. That was the deal, and it worked.

Some practical matters, while I have your attention. The password situation did not improve on its own; sorry. The photos are in three places and none of them are labeled — but you will know the faces, and that is what matters. Call the old friend. Check the coat pockets of the winter jacket before you donate it. And whatever the small daily pleasure is in 2036 — the walk, the song, the cup of something warm — I hope we never got too busy for it. We were busy here, too, and it was never once the point.

Why write such a letter at all? Because the future self is the easiest person in the world to forget and the only one we are guaranteed to meet. A letter turns 'someday' into a correspondence. It proves, in your own handwriting, that someone in 2026 was rooting for you by name. When 2036 feels ordinary — and it will, the way every year does from inside — that proof can land like a flare.

So seal the envelope. Write 'Open in 2036' on the front, and put it somewhere the decade can find it: a drawer, a book, a friend who keeps promises. Then go and live the ten years that make it interesting. I will be doing the writing. You do the arriving. Between us, we have it covered — with time.

“A letter to the future is a message in a bottle thrown forward instead of out to sea — and the shore it washes up on is your own life.”
Your turn

If you wrote one letter tonight to yourself in 2036, what is the first honest sentence you would put in it?
