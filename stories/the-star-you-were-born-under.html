The Star You Were Born Under

The light of your birth star left long before you existed. On the night you were born, the sky was already telling a story about you.

With Time · November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Somewhere, tonight, there is a star whose light left its surface before you were born, before your parents were born, before the town you live in had a name. That light is still traveling. It will reach someone's eyes a hundred years from now, and they will think it is happening now. The sky is a letter that takes centuries to arrive.

On the night you were born, the sky was arranged in a pattern that will never happen again in exactly that way. The planets were in their places. The Moon was at its phase. A satellite crossed over the hospital, or the house, or the village — too small and too fast for anyone to notice, but there, keeping its appointment. You arrived under a sky that belonged only to that night.

Astronomers can tell you which stars were overhead. They can run the sky backwards like a film and show you the constellations of your first hour. It is a strange feeling, seeing them: these are the stars that watched you arrive, and they have not changed since. People change. Towns change. The stars do not. They are the only witnesses to your whole life who were there at the start and will be there at the end.

Maybe that is why we look up when we are sad, or lost, or far from home. Not for answers, but for continuity. The sky is the one thing that does not ask you to be anyone else. It was there before you, and it will be there after you, and on the night you were born it was exactly, briefly, yours.

Tonight, go outside and look up. Somewhere in that crowd of light is the star you were born under. Its light is old, but it is still arriving. So are you.

“We are not separate from the night sky. We are its slowest, warmest light, briefly walking around.”
Your turn

Do you know which stars were overhead on the night you were born — and does it change anything to know they are still there?
