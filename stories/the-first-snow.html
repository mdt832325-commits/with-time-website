The First Snow

It always snowed on the night before the first day of school holidays — or at least, that is how every child in the village remembers it.

With Time · November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

It always snowed on the night before the holidays — or at least, that is how every child in the village remembers it. The grown-ups insist it snowed on ordinary Tuesdays too, on the wrong weeks, sometimes not at all. But memory is not a weather report. Memory is the snow as it looked from a small window, with the whole long holiday ahead of you, and your breath fogging the glass.

The first snow of the year had a sound, or rather, it had a silence. The village went quiet in a way it never was in summer — the carts, the dogs, the radios all muffled, as if the world had been wrapped in something soft. Children pressed their faces to windows and watched the flakes come down, each one different, each one brief, and understood, without being told, that winter had arrived and with it, freedom.

In the morning, the world was new. The lane was white. The rooftops were white. The old bicycle leaning against the wall wore a white coat. Breakfast tasted better on the first snow morning — nobody knows why, but every person who grew up in that village will tell you it is true. Then the door opened, and the cold hit, and the shouting began, and the snowball war started before anyone had even finished their tea.

Years later, far from that village, the first snow still stops you. You are an adult now, with appointments and heating bills, and still, for one second, you are small again, at the window, fogging the glass with your breath. Some memories do not fade. They just wait for the weather.

If it snows tonight, wherever you are, go to the window. The child you were is still there, watching. Say hello.

“The first snow does not fall on the ground. It falls on the year, and covers everything that hurt, just for a while.”

What is your first snow memory — and who was standing beside you when you saw it?
